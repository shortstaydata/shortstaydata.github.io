What we do

We check live listings against the city's own register.

Many U.S. cities require a registration or license for stays under 30 nights, and a public list of those records. We search live short-term rental listings and record whether we found an active, matching registration.

Hotel groups and hotel associations use this to see listings that compete with hotels for the same short-stay guests. Unions, apartment and condo associations, researchers, and city stakeholders use the counts and neighborhood breakdowns. Building owners use it to spot short-term rentals they never approved, before those listings become a liability.

We say what the records show, such as "no active City registration" or "no matching registration found." We do not decide whether a rule was broken. Some listings may be exempt, or registered under another name.

Our edge

We find the listings other tools miss.

Hosts often pin listings in a neighboring city or a few miles away. We match listings from their photos, descriptions and building details, not just their map pin, then check them against public registration records.

Mislabeled listings

A unit in one neighborhood can appear on Airbnb as a different city. Searches by city or by radius never surface it. Ours do.

Beyond city lines

City enforcement vendors often look only inside their own borders, and other tools search a fixed radius. We start from the listings and the register, and work outward.

Counts, reports and monitoring

You can request a citywide count, a neighborhood breakdown, a full dated report, or ongoing monitoring when new listings appear.

Facts only

We report what public records show.
