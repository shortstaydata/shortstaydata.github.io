Why we started

Owners can be held responsible for listings they didn't know about.

We started Short Stay Data to help building owners avoid unfair liability. Under LA's Home-Sharing ordinance (LAMC 12.22 A.32), the City can cite property owners, not just the hosts who post the listings. Fines run about $605.73 per day per listing and rise after 120 days.

In 2025, the owner of a Hollywood apartment building paid $150,000 to settle with the City despite saying it didn't know about the rentals. Read the Los Angeles Times story.

The practical answer is to know what's listed in your building, document it, and act quickly.

Our edge

We find the listings other tools miss.

Hosts often pin listings in a neighboring city or a few miles away. We match listings to your building from their photos, descriptions and building details, not just their map pin.

Mislabeled listings

A unit in Koreatown can appear on Airbnb as "Culver City." Searches by city or by radius never surface it. Ours do.

Beyond city lines

City enforcement vendors look only inside their own city's borders, and landlord tools search a fixed radius. We start from your building and work outward.

One evidence package

You get one building-level report covering every listing and host account we find, with sources and dates for each one.

Facts only

We report what public records show.

Our reports describe listings, addresses and City records. We don't make accusations or legal determinations, and we don't collect or sell hosts' personal details.

A step you can take today, for free

LA property owners can file the City's Property Owner Proactive Prohibition through the Home-Sharing Application and Registration Portal (HARP). Once it's filed, tenants can't register units at the property for Home-Sharing.
