How it works

From a live listing to a dated registration check.

We search short-term rental listings, check them against public city registration records, and give you a dated count or report. We say what the records show. We don't decide whether a rule was broken.

Step 1

We search live listings

We review public listings on major short-term rental platforms that advertise stays under 30 nights, including listings pinned in a different city or neighborhood.

Step 2

We check city registration records

We match each listing against the city's public registration records and record whether we found an active, matching registration.

Step 3

You get a dated report

That can be a citywide count, a neighborhood breakdown, or a full report with the listing, the registration check and the date we checked it.

Step 4

Optional monitoring

Listings come and go. With monitoring we keep checking, alert you when a new listing appears, and send a dated record of what we found and what was removed.

Our standards

Verifiable facts, no personal data.

Sourced and dated

Every finding cites a public source and the date we checked it, so anyone can verify it.

No host personal details

We report on listings, areas and public records. We don't compile or sell hosts' names, contact details or profiles.

No accusations

We say what the records show, such as "no active City registration" or "no matching registration found." Only the city decides whether a violation occurred.

Public sources only

We don't access private or password-protected data, and we don't contact hosts or guests.

Human-reviewed

A person reviews close matches. If we can't confirm a listing belongs where it appears to, we say so or leave it out.

For building owners

From a building address to a dated evidence report.

You give us an address. We find what's listed there, including listings pinned in a different neighborhood or city, and check them against city records. Hosts often pin a listing a few miles away. We match from photos, descriptions and building details, not just the map pin.

Search and match

We review public listings in and around the area, then match each one to the building using its own public text, photos, amenities, layout and reviews.

Check city and building records

For each listing we check the city's public registration records. Where the city publishes them, we can also pull public parcel, unit-count, zoning and related housing records.

Get a dated report

One report for the building, covering every listing we can confirm. Each finding includes the listing link, how we matched it, the registration check and the date checked.

Optional monitoring

With monitoring we check daily, alert you the same day a new listing appears, and send a dated monthly record of what we found and what was removed.

What an owner can do with the report

  • Match units from your own records. Listings rarely show unit numbers. Photos, layout and parking details usually let your team identify the unit.
  • Follow your lease process. Your property manager can use the report when addressing the issue with residents.
  • Keep a record. A dated report shows when you learned about a listing and what you did next.

We don't offer legal advice, and a report isn't legal protection. It gives you the facts so you can document the issue and act.

FAQ

Questions people ask

What does a "no active or matching city registration" finding mean?

It means we did not find an active registration in the city's public records that matches the listing. Some listings may be exempt, or registered under another name. We report what the records show. The city decides whether a rule was broken.

Do you name buildings or hosts on this site?

No. Public pages do not name buildings or hosts. A report goes to the person who requested it.
