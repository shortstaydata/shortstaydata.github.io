How it works

From a building address to a dated evidence report.

You give us an address. We find what's listed there, check it against City records, and document everything so you or your property manager can act.

Step 1

We search and match listings to your building

We review public listings on major short-term rental platforms in and around your area, including listings pinned in a different city. Each one is matched to your building using its own public text, photos, amenities, layout and reviews.

Step 2

We check City and County records

For each listing we check the City of Los Angeles Home-Sharing registration records. For the building we pull public records: parcel number, unit count, zoning and rent stabilization (RSO) status.

Step 3

You get a dated evidence report

One report for the building, covering every listing and host account we found. Each finding includes the listing link, how we matched it, the registration check result and the date checked. It's ready to hand to your property manager or the City.

Step 4

Optional monitoring

Listings come and go. With monitoring we check daily, alert you the same day a new listing appears, and send a dated monthly record of what we found and what was removed.

What you can do with the report

  • Match units from your own records. Listings rarely show unit numbers. Photos, layout and parking details usually let your team identify the unit.
  • Follow your lease process. Your property manager can use the report when addressing the issue with residents.
  • File the City's prohibition. Owners can file the Property Owner Proactive Prohibition through the City's HARP portal so tenants can't register units for Home-Sharing.
  • Keep a record. A dated report shows when you learned about a listing and what you did next.

We don't offer legal advice, and a report isn't legal protection. It gives you the facts quickly so you can document the issue and act.

Our standards

Verifiable facts, no personal data.

Sourced and dated

Every finding cites a public source and the date we checked it, so anyone can verify it.

No host personal details

We report on listings, addresses and public records. We don't compile or sell hosts' names, contact details or profiles.

No accusations

We say what the records show, such as "no matching registration found." Only the City decides whether a violation occurred.

Public sources only

We don't access private or password-protected data, and we don't contact hosts or guests.

Human-reviewed

A person reviews every match. If we can't confirm a listing is in your building, we say so or leave it out.

Corrections

Anyone, hosts included, can email contact@shortstaydata.com with a correction. If we got something wrong, we fix it.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What is Home-Sharing? Home-Sharing is the City of Los Angeles term for renting all or part of a host's own primary residence as a short-term rental, meaning a stay of 30 consecutive days or less. It requires a City registration. City-approved hotels, motels, bed and breakfasts and Transient Occupancy Residential Structures are not short-term rentals under the ordinance. (Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines, 2026; as of October 2026.)

Can a property owner be held responsible for a tenant's short-term rental?

Yes. The City's Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines state that a property owner may be held responsible for Home-Sharing violations occurring on their property. The City also states that it does not mediate disputes between property owners and tenants about Home-Sharing.

Source: Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines (2026), Section IX. As of October 2026.

Can a tenant do Home-Sharing without the owner's approval?

No. Under the ordinance, a renter or lessee may not engage in Home-Sharing without the landlord's prior written approval, and must give the City a copy of that approval when applying for registration.

Source: Home-Sharing Ordinance No. 185,931. As of October 2026.

Can a rent-stabilized (RSO) unit be used for Home-Sharing?

No. A proposed short-term rental cannot be subject to the Rent Stabilization Ordinance (LAMC Chapter 15). The City notes that a property's RSO status can be reviewed in ZIMAS under the Housing tab.

Source: Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines (2026), Section III. As of October 2026.

How many nights a year can a registered host rent out a unit?

A Regular Home-Sharing Registration allows up to 120 days in a calendar year. Going beyond 120 days requires an Extended Home-Sharing Registration.

Source: Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines (2026), Sections II and VI. As of October 2026.

Does a listing have to show a registration number?

Yes. The guidelines prohibit advertising Home-Sharing without a valid registration number issued to that host, and require every advertisement to clearly list it.

Source: Home-Sharing Administrative Guidelines (2026), Section VI. As of October 2026.

How can an owner stop tenants from registering units for Home-Sharing?

The owner of record can file the Property Owner's Proactive Prohibition through the City's HARP portal. The form requires a notarized, hand-drawn signature, and each property needs its own form. It does not revoke an approval the owner has already given; that requires a separate revocation form.

Source: City Planning, Property Owner's Proactive Prohibition form. As of October 2026.
